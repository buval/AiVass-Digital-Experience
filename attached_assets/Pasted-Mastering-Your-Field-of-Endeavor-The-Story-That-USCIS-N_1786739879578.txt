Mastering Your Field of Endeavor. The Story That USCIS Needs to Hear
A comprehensive guide for extraordinary professionals navigating the EB-1A process


If you're pursuing an EB-1A green card (the "extraordinary ability" category), you've likely encountered the phrase "field of endeavor" dozens of times. It appears in USCIS guidance, petition letters, and RFE responses and yet it remains one of the most misunderstood concepts in immigration law.

Here's the truth: Your field of endeavor is not your job title. It's not your SOC codes (Standard Occupational Classification). It's not even your industry.

Your field of endeavor is the narrative thread that connects your past achievements, current work, and future contributions to the United States. It's the story of what you've mastered, where you've excelled, and why America needs your expertise.

This guide breaks down everything you need to know—whether you're a machine learning engineer, a classical violinist, a biotech researcher, a product marketer, or a professional athlete. By the end, you'll know exactly how to identify, articulate, and defend your field of endeavor.


Part 1: What Is a "Field of Endeavor"? (In Plain English)
The Official Definition
According to USCIS guidance, your field of endeavor is the specific area of expertise in which you have demonstrated extraordinary ability. It's the domain where you've achieved sustained national or international acclaim.

The Real Definition
Think of your field of endeavor as:

The intersection of:

What you're exceptionally good at (your expertise)

What you've been recognized for (your achievements)

What you'll continue contributing (your future impact)

NOT:

Your current job title

Your college major

Your company's industry classification

A single skill or tool you use

Why This Matters
USCIS uses your field of endeavor to evaluate:

Consistency: Do your achievements tell a coherent story?

Extraordinary Ability: Have you risen to the top of this specific field?

Future Benefit: Will you continue working in this field in the U.S.?

Qualification: Do you meet the criteria within this field?

Part 6: SOC Codes vs. Reality
Understanding SOC (Standard Occupational Classification) Codes
Many applicants worry: "My I-140 petition lists SOC code 15-1252 (Software Developer). Does this mean I can't become a CTO?"

The answer: SOC codes are administrative tools, not handcuffs.

What SOC Codes Actually Do
SOC codes help USCIS:

Understand your occupation in standardized terms

Verify that your job exists in the U.S. labor market

Process your petition administratively

SOC codes do NOT:

Lock you into a specific job title forever

Prevent career advancement

Override your actual field of endeavor

Define the scope of your extraordinary ability

Part 7: Common Mistakes to Avoid
❌ Mistake #1: Defining Your Field by Your Company
Wrong: "My field of endeavor is marketing at Salesforce."

Right: "My field of endeavor is enterprise software marketing, with specialization in customer retention strategies."

Why: Your field must be portable across employers.


❌ Mistake #2: Being Too Broad
Wrong: "My field of endeavor is technology."

Right: "My field of endeavor is cloud infrastructure security."

Why: You cannot demonstrate extraordinary ability in all of "technology." You need a specific domain where you've risen to the top.


❌ Mistake #3: Being Too Narrow
Wrong: "My field of endeavor is implementing OAuth 2.0 authentication protocols in React applications."

Right: "My field of endeavor is application security architecture."

Why: Too narrow = not enough evidence, not enough peers, not enough room to grow.


❌ Mistake #4: Confusing Tools with Field
Wrong: "My field of endeavor is Tableau and data visualization."

Right: "My field of endeavor is business intelligence and data analytics."

Why: Tools change. Fields endure. Tableau might be replaced, but data analytics will remain relevant.


❌ Mistake #5: Ignoring Career Pivots
Wrong: Pretending you've always done exactly the same thing.

Right: Explaining how your pivot demonstrates deepening expertise.

Example: "While my job title changed from Software Engineer to Product Manager, my field of endeavor has consistently been improving user experience in mobile applications—first through code, now through product strategy."


❌ Mistake #6: Multiple Unrelated Fields
Wrong: "I'm extraordinary in machine learning, classical piano, and real estate investment."

Right: Pick the field with the strongest evidence and clearest future path.

Why: USCIS expects sustained acclaim in one field, not scattered achievements across multiple unrelated domains.


Part 8: Crafting Your Field of Endeavor Statement
The Formula
Use this template to articulate your field:

"My field of endeavor is [CORE DOMAIN], with specialization in [SPECIFIC EXPERTISE] as applied to [CONTEXT/APPLICATION]."

Examples Using the Formula
Tech: "My field of endeavor is machine learning engineering, with specialization in natural language processing as applied to healthcare applications."

Business: "My field of endeavor is digital marketing strategy, with specialization in performance marketing as applied to e-commerce brands."

Science: "My field of endeavor is structural biology, with specialization in cryo-electron microscopy as applied to drug discovery."

Arts: "My field of endeavor is documentary filmmaking, with specialization in environmental storytelling as applied to climate change narratives."

Education: "My field of endeavor is educational technology, with specialization in adaptive learning platforms as applied to K-12 mathematics."

✅ An immigration officer

✅ A reporter

✅ Your grandmother

✅ A peer in your field

If yes, you've nailed it.

Red Flags to Avoid
🚩 Past and present don't align Example: Past = cancer research, Present = marketing analytics Solution: Choose one field and emphasize the strongest evidence

🚩 Future work is unrelated to achievements Example: Achievements = graphic design, Future = starting a restaurant Solution: Explain how skills transfer OR choose a different field

🚩 Gap between present and future Example: Present = employed as data scientist, Future = "I might do consulting" Solution: Show concrete plans (job offer, research position, entrepreneurship with clear focus)


Part 10: Best Practices for Petition Letters
How Attorneys Should Frame Your Field
Weak Opening: "The beneficiary is a software engineer working in technology."

Strong Opening: "The beneficiary is a recognized expert in machine learning infrastructure, specifically the optimization of large-scale neural networks for production environments. Her field of endeavor is ML systems engineering."

Using Field-Specific Language
Throughout your petition, consistently reference your field:

In the opening: "Ms. Chen has achieved extraordinary recognition in the field of computational biology..."

When discussing criteria: "Within the field of computational biology, Ms. Chen's 45 citations place her in the top 5% of researchers..."

In the recommendation letters: "I have followed Ms. Chen's work in computational biology for five years..."

In the future plans: "Ms. Chen will continue her work in computational biology at Stanford University..."

Dealing with Interdisciplinary Work
Many professionals work at intersections. Here's how to frame it:

Option 1: Choose the Primary Field "While my work draws on both neuroscience and computer science, my field of endeavor is computational neuroscience—the application of computational methods to understanding brain function."

Option 2: Acknowledge the Intersection "My field of endeavor sits at the intersection of materials science and renewable energy, specifically the development of next-generation battery technologies."

Key: Pick one frame and stick to it throughout the entire petition.

Part 12: Frequently Asked Questions
Q1: Can my field of endeavor be interdisciplinary?
A: Yes, but frame it carefully. Choose a primary field that has:

An established community of practitioners

Recognition mechanisms (awards, conferences, publications)

Clear criteria for extraordinary ability

Example: "Bioinformatics" or "Computational Biology" rather than "I work in both biology and computer science."


Q2: I changed industries completely. Can I still make this work?
A: Sometimes. Ask yourself:

Did your core expertise transfer?

Can you frame both roles under one broader field?

Do you have recognition in the new field?

Example that works: Software Engineer → Product Manager Field: "User-centric product development for consumer applications"

Example that's difficult: Chemical Engineer → Real Estate Investor (No obvious field connects these)


Q3: Should I use academic language or everyday language?
A: Use professional language that's accessible but precise.

Too academic: "My field of endeavor is the sociotechnical implications of algorithmic bias in machine learning systems deployed at scale."

Too casual: "I'm really good at making AI not be racist."

Just right: "My field of endeavor is ethical AI development, specifically addressing bias in machine learning systems."


Q4: My petition was denied because USCIS said my field was too broad. What now?
A: In your response:

Narrow the definition with specific expertise areas

Provide more context about what the field includes/excludes

Show clear boundaries by referencing specific communities, journals, conferences

Reframe evidence to emphasize depth in the narrower field

Before: "My field is marketing." After: "My field is growth marketing for B2B SaaS companies, specifically customer acquisition and retention strategies for enterprise software."


Q5: I have achievements in two separate fields. Which should I choose?
A: Choose the field where you have:

More recent recognition

Stronger evidence across multiple criteria

Clearer future plans in the U.S.

Better recommendation letters

One strong field beats two weak fields.


Q6: Can I work in a different field after my green card is approved?
A: Technically yes—the green card doesn't restrict your job. However:

Your I-140 petition is tied to your stated field and future plans

Changing fields immediately after approval could raise questions

It's safest to continue in your field for at least 1-2 years post-approval


Q7: What if my field is very new or emerging?
A: Emerging fields can work if you can demonstrate:

Community: Are there others in this field? Conferences? Publications?

Recognition mechanisms: How is extraordinary ability measured?

Precedent: Has anyone else received an EB-1A in this field?

Examples of emerging fields that have worked:

Prompt engineering for large language models

NFT art and digital collectibles

TikTok marketing and short-form video strategy

CRISPR gene editing applications

Key: Show that it's a field (with practitioners and standards), not just a trend or tool.

Part 14: Advanced Strategies
Strategy #1: The "Zooming" Technique
When USCIS challenges your field definition, you can either:

Zoom Out (make it broader):

Use when accused of being too narrow

Example: "Machine learning for fraud detection" → "Applied machine learning in financial technology"

Zoom In (make it narrower):

Use when accused of being too broad

Example: "Marketing" → "Performance marketing for direct-to-consumer e-commerce brands"

Strategy #2: The Comparison Frame
Position your field by comparison to what it's NOT:

"My field of endeavor is computational linguistics—the intersection of computer science and language—not general software engineering or theoretical linguistics. I focus specifically on how machines process human language."

This helps USCIS understand boundaries.

Strategy #3: The Historical Anchor
Reference established terminology:

"My field of endeavor is what the National Science Foundation classifies as 'Human-Computer Interaction' (HCI)—the study and design of interactions between people and computers."

Using recognized frameworks adds credibility.

Strategy #4: The Future-Backward Approach
Start with where you want to go:

Identify your dream role in the U.S.

Define the field that role operates in

Work backward to show how your past fits this field

Reframe current role to bridge past and future

Example:

Future Role: AI Ethics Lead at tech company

Field: Ethical AI development and algorithmic fairness

Past: ML engineer → Reframe as "building fair ML systems"

Present: ML team lead → Reframe as "leading ethical AI initiatives"
